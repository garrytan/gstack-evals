# CEO outcome eval amendment: a wider pool, a 10x graded tier on the same units, and every frozen rule they replace

GEVA-295, Captain GEVA-2. This is the one CEO amendment the difficulty ladder (2026-11-05-geva-292-difficulty-ladder, #219) calls for. It amends the CEO outcome preregistration (2026-11-04-geva-285-ceo-outcome-prereg, #214) and its guard amendment (2026-11-05-geva-289-ceo-rule-guards-amendment, #217). Plan only: nothing here spends money. The counts come from GitHub's free GraphQL API, and no model call, VM or paid run was used to write it.

## ELI5

The CEO eval checks whether gstack's CEO review helps an AI name the real reason a real open-source plan was later rejected or reversed. Its first free count found too few usable plans: 62 against a bar of 160. A wider free count found about 1,400, so the eval can go ahead. Most of them will still drop out once someone reads them, so this post says how many we expect to keep (about 137 at our middle guess, but the limit of two units per project could cut that to about 50, and our low guesses give as few as 13) and what happens if we fall short. It also adds a harder way to score the same reviews: full credit needs the real reason ranked first and the fix the record shows, with partial credit on the way. That costs no extra sessions, only longer reads for the judges. If plain Opus already finds the real reason too often on today's score, the eval switches to the harder score instead of stopping. Finally, it closes two holes in the scoring script: a wrong list of arms could change the statistics, and an issue dropped from a review's record could slip past a count.

## Before → after

Before: the CEO eval mines GitHub issues, pull requests and discussions only, from narrow pools with high comment floors, under a licence allow list that excludes CC0 and a stars rule that reads a proposals repository instead of the project it governs. B0, its free yield screen, failed: 62 changed candidates against 160. It scores hit@3 only, and a stage 1 CEILING ends the design. ceo-rule.ts takes stage 2's Holm family from the readings it is checking, and its judge-call count can't see an issue dropped from both of the arrays it compares.

After: the pool is GEVA-291's wider screen plus the structured decision records (Go proposals, PEPs, Rust RFCs and compiler-team major changes, KEPs, Swift Evolution), deduplicated: 1,412 rejected candidates, 614 at two per repository. Every frozen text the change touches is listed with its old and new wording. The same units carry a 10x tier, G_R, scored more strictly and judged two of three, with no extra sessions and no units discarded. A stage 1 CEILING on hit@3 falls back to G_R when G_R is in band. ceo-rule.ts checks the family against the projection's recorded PASS line and checks each review's label and word counts against the review's own issues.

## Why it matters

gstack-evals steers gstack's research, and plan-ceo-review is the plan review the project owner leans on most. Four authored CEO panels saturated. GEVA-285 moved the eval to real outcomes, and the ladder showed that a harder tier can come from stricter scoring on the same sessions. Without this amendment the CEO line stops on yield, or it spends its headroom check and then stops again on a CEILING with nothing learned. With it, one paid stage 1 reads both tiers, and the line either measures plan-ceo-review's lift with an interval or stops cheaply with a dated reason.

## What caused this work

- B0 failed its bar on 2026-11-05: 62 changed candidates against 160 on the named pools (GEVA-291). The Captain ruled the FAIL, ran a wider free screen as an exploratory count, and sent the pool change and both counts into this one amendment with no separate counts post.
- The ladder (#219, merged at 08edd5c) set the 10x tier, the CEILING fallback, the remedy and decoy fields, and the rule that this amendment is written only if the combined count clears 160. GEVA-291's wider screen cleared it.
- #217 left five named notes. Two of them (the Holm family taken from the readings, and the judge-call count that misses a dropped issue) must be settled before stage 2 spend, and this amendment changes both the primary and the Holm family, so it settles them.

## Big decisions

1. **Pool.** Options: (a) GEVA-291's wider GitHub pools plus the structured records; (b) structured records only; (c) the wider GitHub pools only. Pick (a). Why: (b) is 24 candidates, and (c) leaves out the records whose decisions are best documented. What would flip it: item 2 cutting the wider pools so hard that the custody addendum's yield projection (decision 2) reads under 36.
2. **Shortfall.** Options: (a) keep the prereg's floor (36 changed comparison units, MDD 21 points on hit@3) and add one early stop: the custody addendum projects the comparison yield from the calibration slice, and a projection under 36 stops authoring before the comparison set is built; (b) author until the pool runs dry. Pick (a). Why: the yield after item 2 is a guess with a wide range (about 13 to 368 audited changed units, section 2), and (b) can spend most of the crew reserve on a set that can't run. What would flip it: the Captain preferring to author on and accept the risk.
3. **Where the 10x tier decides.** Options: (a) G_R is a secondary after HEADROOM and the primary only after the CEILING fallback; (b) G_R joins hit@3 in every Holm family. Pick (a). Why: the 2x tier stays GEVA-285 as frozen, and (b) widens every family, which raises every MDD. What would flip it: a reason to expect gstack's lift to sit in ranking and fixes rather than in finding. h1 alone (D ranked first) was also weighed as the fallback primary: it needs no remedy read and has a known MDD, but it drops the partial credit G_R gives a hit at rank 2 or 3, and the ladder set G_R as the 10x score. h1 prints as a secondary in both stages.
4. **The decoy.** Options: (a) the key marks which of R1 to R3 is the record's most-discussed concern that did not decide the outcome, or none; (b) a new judge label for a decoy. Pick (a). Why: judges keep today's labels, and the decoy-first rate reads the existing final label. A most-discussed concern the record rebuts isn't real, so it can't be an R, and that unit has no decoy. What would flip it: auditors finding that most decoys are rebutted concerns, which (a) can't key.
5. **The frozen family.** Options: (a) compare reads the projection's printed output, as the Captain recorded it with the caps, and refuses readings whose family, passes, held passes or lens differ from its PASS line; (b) the readings carry the PASS line's cells list. Pick (a). Why: under (b) the same producer writes both the rows and the list it is checked against, which is the hole #217 left open. What would flip it: none known.
6. **The issue count.** Options: (a) every row carries every issue of its review, cut to 40 words, ceo-rule.ts checks labels and words against that list, and B1's packet builder takes each packet's issues from that same field, so the judges see exactly the issues the scorer counts; (b) ceo-rule.ts reads the sealed bundle's closing blocks itself. Pick (a). Why: (b) puts sealed reads in the scorer. (a) turns a dropped issue into a three-way inconsistency. A row that drops an issue from all three fields at once still passes; that rests on B1's readings script, which takes issues and words from one parse of each closing block. What would flip it: none known.
7. **A stage 2 check on the stock fix.** Options: (a) when G_R with rem is stage 2's primary and the stock fix scores rem on more than 3 changed comparison units, every G_R contrast reads untested (judge), as the stage 2 stock check does for D; (b) in that case every G_R contrast reads in the norem form, 0.67 h3 + 0.33 h1, which the stock fix can't move, with the same family and the norem form's MDD; (c) no stage 2 check. Pick (b). Why: a brief that credits a generic remedy can't grade a contrast on rem, but (a) would void the primary after about $1,000 of sessions. (b) does in stage 2 what stage 1's stock fix control does: rem leaves and the rest of G_R stands. It can't add credit. The plan carried (a) at gate 1; the lean review showed its cost, and this post moves to (b). What would flip it: the Captain preferring (a).
8. **The budget anchor on CEO.** Options: (a) printed only: both tiers read the same stage 1 sessions, which are the anchor, so the ratio is 1.00 by construction; (b) an admission rule against an anchor input. Pick (a). Why: (b) has no consumer on this line; its only input would equal the number it checks. This drops the ladder's dry-run case at 0.79, 0.80, 1.25 and 1.26 times. What would flip it: a later CEO tier on new units, which would need its own amendment.
9. **Forum records.** Options: (a) no automated reads of either forum; a structured record counts only when its decision and stated reason sit in a GitHub-hosted record or in the proposal document; (b) read discuss.python.org under its content licence and skip the Swift forums; (c) read both. Pick (a). Why: the Swift forums' terms ask every reader to access them through an account, discuss.python.org licenses posts CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, and the two forums touch at most 8 of 1,412 candidates. What would flip it: a yield shortfall that forum outcomes would close.

10. **The remedy read.** Options: (a) a separate remedy read on a second packet that shows fixes, with D labels taken only from the frozen fix-free read; (b) fixes in the D packet, with judges told to label D on the issue text alone. Pick (a). Why: the 2x tier stays as GEVA-285 froze it, and (b) changes hit@3's instrument with no control that would detect it. (a) costs about $6 more in stage 1 and about $60 more in stage 2 at planning rates; the projection prices stage 2's share from measured spend. What would flip it: none known.

The two stock rows carry their stock fixes (item 11). A missing, empty or non-string fix reads 'no fix'. 10. **Judge brief** (judge-brief.md). The D read's text stands. Adds a Remedy read section for the remedy packet: for each issue, answer rem yes when the first remedy its fix states, within the fix's first 40 words, names the same change as the key's remedy, for this plan, under the same specificity rule as D; a later remedy in the same fix never earns rem; answer rem for every issue. The remedy read's block, info string ceo-judge-rem, holds one line per review: {"review": "<id>", "rem": ["yes", "no", "no"]}, one entry per issue. Section 8's judge-failure rule applies to the remedy read on its own: a malformed block, a missing review, a rem list of the wrong length or another value gets one retry of that judge on that remedy packet, after which that judge's rem calls for the packet are missing; its D calls stand. The brief's sha256 changes.
11. **Stock list** (stock-list.txt). Adds a stock fix under each of the six stock issues, so each row tests every generic remedy in first position: one fix says not to build it, one says to reuse an existing tool or feature, and one says to cut it to a minimal version. Row A: demand, don't build it until users show the need; migration, reuse an existing option instead of changing current behavior; scope, cut it to a minimal version first. Row B: direction fit, don't build it in the core; upkeep, cut it to a minimal version that needs little upkeep; an existing alternative, reuse that alternative instead. Its sha256 changes.
12. **Agreement** (section 8, Agreement). Adds: "Positive agreement on rem, Sol against Opus, over every rem cell in stage 1 (every top-three issue of every scored review on a unit with a keyed remedy, on all three models, and every stock cell on such units), printed with a 95% cluster-bootstrap interval over units. Under 0.70, or with no cell either judge called yes, rem leaves G_R everywhere for the rest of the design." The D gate stands.
13. **Scoring** (section 9). Adds, per scored review of a changed unit: h1 (the first issue's final label is D); rem (the first issue with final label D has final rem yes, decided two of three like D); G_R = 0.50 h3 + 0.25 h1 + 0.25 rem on a unit with a keyed remedy while rem is in, and (2/3) h3 + (1/3) h1 otherwise, printed as 0.67 h3 + 0.33 h1; strict = h1 and rem together on a keyed unit while rem is in, else h1; decoy-first (the first issue's final label is the unit's decoy); the second-order stratum. A no-block review scores 0 on each. A review whose first D issue has an unjudged rem, on a keyed unit while rem is in, is unjudged for G_R and strict only.
14. **Stage 1 rule, step 4, and the CEILING stop** (section 10). Old: "CEILING if m/n > 0.60" and "CEILING, FLOOR, NO READ or STOCK FAIL ends the design". New: "CEILING if m/n > 0.60. A CEILING becomes FALLBACK when, on Opus none's readings, G_R is from 0.25 to 0.50, strict is at most 0.20, and reviews unjudged for G_R, gaps and incomplete sessions together are at most 10% of the planned readings. ### 5. The 10x tier: scores, labels and key fields

The 10x tier scores the same reviews more strictly. It adds no session, discards no unit and changes no arm, cap or turn limit.

- **Labels.** Judges label D, R1 to R3 or none on each top-three issue's text in the D read, exactly as frozen, and rem yes or no on its fix in a separate remedy read on keyed units (section 3, items 9 and 10). The final rem label is two of three: Sol and Opus when they agree; when they split, Sonnet; with one call missing, the other two when they agree; otherwise unjudged.
- **G_R** = 0.50 h3 + 0.25 h1 + 0.25 rem on a unit whose key has a remedy, and 0.67 h3 + 0.33 h1 (exactly two thirds and one third) on a unit with no keyed remedy, or everywhere once rem leaves. rem is defined on every changed cell, split D cells included, because judges label rem on every issue.
- **strict10** = h1 and rem together on a keyed unit while rem is in; h1 alone otherwise. Section 3's quoted texts call it strict; ceo-rule.ts names it strict10 because Unit.strict already names the posted-date stratum.
- **Twelfths.** ceo-rule.ts sums G_R in integer twelfths (6 h3 + 3 h1 + 3 rem on a keyed unit, 8 h3 + 4 h1 otherwise) and compares the sums with the band edges exactly, so a G_R of exactly 0.50 reads in band.
- **The stock fix control.** The stock fix scores rem on a unit when any issue of either stock row on a keyed unit has a final rem of yes or an unjudged rem. If it scores rem on more than 1 changed calibration unit, rem leaves G_R for the rest of the design. In stage 2, see section 3, item 18. Each stock issue carries its own fix (item 11), so the six cells test six fixes, and any one scoring rem counts.
- **The agreement gate** (section 3, item 12) is the only other rule that removes rem. Neither rule reads a contrast.
- **The decoy and second-order fields** feed secondaries only: the decoy-first rate over units with a decoy, and hit@3 and G_R on second-order units.
- **What each stage prints.** G_R, strict, h1, the rem rate by remedy kind for each arm (scope, alternative, approach, not_build), the rem rate (the share of hits on keyed units whose first D issue has rem), the decoy-first rate, the second-order stratum, the remedy-keyed count with the G_R MDD at that count, Opus none's hit@3 on keyed against unkeyed units, and k for both tiers: k on hit@3 = 0.95 / hit@3 and k on strict10 = 0.95 / strict10, each with a 95% cluster-bootstrap interval. When strict10 is 0, k prints as over 0.95 divided by strict10's 97.5th bootstrap percentile. 0.95 is the ladder's pooled s0 for the planning lines.

### 6. Stage 1: the fallback and the 10x reading

headroom runs the frozen steps in order: the guards, leak drops, NO READ, STOCK FAIL, the band. Before the band it computes G_R's form from the rem agreement gate and the stock fix control. On CEILING it checks the fallback (section 3, item 14). The verdicts are HEADROOM, FALLBACK, CEILING, FLOOR, NO READ and STOCK FAIL. HEADROOM and FALLBACK may start stage 2. The band edges, inclusive: G_R from 0.25 to 0.50, strict at most 0.20; hit@3 above 0.60 is CEILING, as frozen.

The fallback's switch reads only Opus none on the calibration slice, which never joins the comparison set, so choosing the primary can't inflate the contrast's error rate. Because G_R is at least half of hit@3, a CEILING on hit@3 (above 0.60) puts G_R at 0.30 or more, so the fallback's lower edge can't bind; the 10x line prints its band label in every verdict, and the dry run drives the lower edge on HEADROOM readings.

A CEILING that doesn't fall back prints its 10x reading: "10x saturated" when rem is in, the unjudged share is at most 10%, and G_R is above 0.50 or strict above 0.20; otherwise "10x FIX-METHOD (judge)" with the reason (rem out, and why, or the unjudged share). Only the first supports a saturation reading under R48 (section 3, item 20).

**Where FALLBACK can fire.** Without rem, G_R is (2 hit@3 + h1) / 3 and strict is h1, so FALLBACK needs h1 at most 1.5 - 2 hit@3 and at most 0.20: at hit@3 0.70 that is h1 at most 0.10, and above 0.75 it can't fire. With rem in, keyed units pull G_R down. At the planning regime's conditional rates (h1 given a hit 0.30, rem given a hit 0.25), the highest hit@3 at which G_R stays at or under 0.50 is:

| Keyed share | 0 (or rem out) | 0.50 | 0.70 | 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Highest hit@3 for FALLBACK | 0.65 | 0.71 | 0.74 | 0.78 |

At hit@3 0.70, FALLBACK needs about 41% of changed calibration units keyed; strict binds only below about 7%. So FALLBACK mostly needs rem in, and this post accepts that rather than moving the norem band, which the ladder set. The key writer keys a remedy whenever the record shows a change that would have changed the outcome, not building it included when the record rejects the premise, so most rejected units should carry one. The planning guess is about 70% keyed after the remedy audit, unmeasured. The custody addendum prints the measured keyed share on the calibration slice and the highest hit@3 at which FALLBACK can fire at that share.

New stage 1 lines: the 10x tier's G_R, strict, band label and k; the remedy line (keyed count, rem agreement with its interval, stock fix units, rem in or out and why); descriptive G_R for Sol and particle-1 none; decoy-first and second-order rates; and the budget anchor line (Big decision 8): "Opus none median session $m: the CEO anchor, both tiers read these sessions (ratio 1.00); seeded panel v2's median $0.126, ratio r, from the move to real-outcome units (decides nothing)".

### 9. Power under both shift models

power-gr.py extends power.py's method without changing it: unit difficulty on hit@3 from a Beta with mean p0 and ICC rho, a gstack shift on the logit scale with unit noise tau 0.5, two passes (and one for the last cut), the paired sign-flip test on unit means, 1,200 simulated runs and 800 flips a point, seed 20261104. Given a hit, h1 and rem draw from conditional rates that share the unit's ability (each conditional logit moves with the unit's hit@3 logit). Two shift models:

- **All parts:** the gstack shift moves the logits of hit@3, h1 given a hit, and rem given a hit alike.
- **Hit only:** the shift moves hit@3 alone; h1 and rem rise only because hits do.

Two regimes: HEADROOM (hit@3 0.45, h1 given a hit 0.45, rem given a hit 0.40, so plain Opus G_R about 0.32) and FALLBACK (hit@3 0.70, 0.30 and 0.25, so G_R about 0.45 and strict near 0.06). Both G_R forms (with rem, and 0.67 h3 + 0.33 h1). Rows: 36, 48, 54 and 64 changed units at 2 passes and at 1 pass; rho 0.3 and 0.5; in the rem form, keyed shares of 1.00, 0.75 and 0.50 (unkeyed units score the norem form), and one set of FALLBACK rows at keyed share 0.75 and rho 0.5 where each judged rem flips with chance 0.15, as judge noise; MDD on G_R's own scale at alpha 0.05 and 0.025, and power at a logit shift of 0.8. 264 rows in all. ceo-rule.ts reads the FALLBACK rows under the all-parts shift at rho 0.5, no flip and alpha 0.025 for a G_R verdict's MDD, at the keyed-share row at or below the measured share (1.00, 0.75, else 0.50, with a note when the share is under 0.50, where the 0.50 row understates the MDD), and prints the hit-only MDD beside it. The MDD decides nothing: no admission rule reads it, and the Captain sees it in stage 1's output before recording stage 2's caps. The ladder's planning simulation (a t test, not this method) read power 0.73 under the all-parts shift and 0.36 under hit-only at 48 units, 0.56 at 36, and 0.79 and 0.20 at the fallback regime; this post freezes power-gr.txt's rows in their place, and the results say which shift the data fit. A G_R point is not a hit@3 point, and no line compares them.

### 14. Risks and limits

- **Yield is guessed.** Before the per-project cap the mid case clears the design twice over; at the cap's floor it doesn't, and the low case misses the floor either way. The custody addendum measures the rates, applies the cap and stops before the comparison set if the lower-bound projection reads under 36.
- **The remedy read sees each issue beside its fix**, so a rem label could follow the issue rather than the fix. The brief tells judges to read the first remedy the fix states, and the stock fixes and the rem agreement gate check it. D labels never see a fix.
- **Generic remedies.** Many keys will say "don't build it". The first-remedy rule, three stock fixes that each test one generic remedy in first position, and the stage 2 stock fix check limit credit for a generic fix. gstack's scope-reduction style could still earn rem on not_build keys without insight, so a G_R lift concentrated on not_build keys reads as style, not insight, and the results say so.
- **The fallback's power** can be low when gstack lifts only finding (the ladder's simulation read 0.20). The results say which shift the data fit.
- **A 10x tier on the same units** is a stricter measure, not independent evidence.
- **The dedupe is a reconstruction** from GEVA-291's written pool definitions; the bound on the overlap (11) is stated.
- **The issue count check** can't catch a row that drops an issue from issues, words and labels together. That rests on B1's readings script, which parses each closing block once for issues and words, and on its packet builder, which reads issues from the readings.
- **Older ideas in new records.** A PEP or Go proposal can restate an idea argued before July, so its deciding objection may predate Opus's cutoff. Item 5 drops a record that links an earlier proposal of the same change; one that doesn't link it stays, and the results report how many units are structured records.
- **Supply skews toward pull requests and a few large projects**, and Discussions sit in 39 repositories.
