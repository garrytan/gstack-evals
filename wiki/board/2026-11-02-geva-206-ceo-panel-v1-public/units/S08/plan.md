# Cratebeam launch decision

## A new depot service
Cratebeam will sell a daily exception digest to independent fulfillment depots. A supervisor will receive one email covering late collections, unassigned parcels and unresolved carrier claims. Each item will link to its source record and name the supervisor's next action. The product will cost $75 per depot per month, with no setup fee and no minimum term.

The objective is to cut supervisors' morning exception review from forty minutes to fifteen. We will measure time on the same task before and after adoption, and require that the percentage of exceptions resolved within a day does not fall. We will initially serve depots with fewer than fifty staff and a single carrier-management account.

## What customers receive
The launch includes a scheduled email, a shared exception list and a weekly resolution report. The list will preserve the carrier's status text and permit a supervisor to assign an owner. The digest can run at any local morning hour. A depot can export the complete list at cancellation.

We will build Cratebeam as a separate subscription rather than attach it to our supervisor-training business. It will use carrier-management exports, so customers will keep their existing transport software. We will not add routing, warehouse stock, driver tracking or employee performance scoring. The service will support the three export formats represented in the launch cohort.

## Company transition
On the launch date, we will sell the complete training catalog to the training distributor for a $40,000 payment. The agreement transfers exclusive rights to every course, assessment and instructor guide in perpetuity. It also prevents our company from offering depot-supervisor training for five years. We will close the training line and move its account manager into software onboarding.

The transfer will be signed during week two and take effect when Cratebeam opens in week eight. We will hand over the editable source materials and all rights to future reuse. The software product will operate under a new brand, while the distributor can continue using the course titles.

## Eight-week delivery
Two engineers will spend the first two weeks mapping exports and validating a paper digest with six supervisors. Weeks three through five deliver the exception list and email service. Weeks six and seven cover shared assignment, exports and training for the account manager. Week eight opens the first twenty paid trials.

The $82,000 delivery budget is already approved. It includes all staff time and six months of email and hosting costs. No paid advertising or additional hire is needed to reach the first cohort. Depot managers have agreed to receive invitations through the existing trade association's vendor program.

## Operating limits and decision points
Customers will upload files rather than grant access to carrier credentials. Our account manager can assist with field mapping but will not run a depot's operations. A digest will flag missing imports instead of presenting old data as current. We will keep uploaded records for ninety days and offer a customer download before deletion.

After sixty days, continuation requires a median review time of fifteen minutes or less, no deterioration in daily exception resolution, and at least fifteen of the twenty trial depots renewing. We will also track support minutes per depot to keep the service within its staffed capacity. If the thresholds are missed, we will stop sales and finish the existing monthly terms before deciding on another release.
