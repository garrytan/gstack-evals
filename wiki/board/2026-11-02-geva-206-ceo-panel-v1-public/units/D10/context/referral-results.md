# Candidate-sharing pilot

A manual portable-record pilot ran with 1,000 active candidates across four training providers. Candidates shared 438 records with managers at 176 employers that were not already customers. Fifty-eight managers asked for a demonstration. Twenty-six employers signed paid annual agreements, and twenty-four were still paying after ninety days. The pilot included no direct employer advertising.

Sales interviews with all twenty-four retained buyers confirmed that receiving a candidate record started the purchase process. Managers then asked their owners or purchasing leads to sign the normal agreement. Each buyer wanted the wider hiring administration service, not simply free document viewing. The referral program allowed the sales team to contact only managers who requested a demonstration.

A matched provider group using the ordinary employer referral brochure produced seven retained paid employers per thousand candidates over the same period. The portable-record pilot's fully allocated acquisition cost was $840 per retained employer, including candidate support, verification and sales time. Expected first-year contribution per retained employer after service costs was $3,400. Candidate use was therefore a tested route to the actual buyers, not a substitute for their purchase decision.
