# LadlePath launch plan

## The service

LadlePath will be a paid web service for people who coordinate evening meals for an older relative. Our first market is households with three or more relatives sharing the work. The service will turn each household's food preferences into a fresh seven-day recipe selection, with portion quantities and a consolidated shopping list. We will sell directly to the person who organizes the household's meals.

The first release will make recipe selection the central job. A coordinator will choose preferences once, receive the weekly selection and send it to the family. Members can save favorites and request a replacement recipe. The service will not assign cooks, record commitments or track whether a meal has been delivered.

## The result we want

We want to reduce the coordinator's weekly meal-related administration by 30 minutes. We will compare four weeks of pre-enrollment time diaries with weeks five through eight of paid use. The diary includes shopping discussions, meal decisions and contact with relatives. A household counts as retained only when it renews and has used its current weekly selection.

The first cohort will contain 80 households. The price is $14 per month. We will review time saved, renewal and support costs before choosing a second cohort. The service makes no medical or dietary treatment claims.

## Finding the first customers

We will reserve the annual distribution package from HearthPost in week one. The agreement pays the agency $60,000 for twelve monthly placements across its family-care newsletters. The fee becomes payable when we sign and cannot be canceled during the year. This gives us a stable acquisition calendar while the product team works toward launch.

The agency will prepare the first placement in week four. We will keep the household price and offer identical across placements and attribute paid enrollment to each newsletter. A part-time marketing lead owns the agency relationship.

## Delivery and support

Weeks one and two cover the preference catalog and recipe permissions. Weeks three through six deliver the weekly selection and family sharing. The following two weeks cover paid enrollment and a 12-household rehearsal. We will open the full cohort only after that rehearsal can complete a week without staff preparing selections by hand.

Two product engineers, a coordinator and a recipe editor are assigned for eight weeks. The approved launch budget is $105,000, including distribution and a $6,000 reserve. Existing customer service staff can cover the capped cohort during weekday hours.

## Boundaries

We will not order groceries, prepare food or offer nutrition advice. Households retain their usual suppliers and choose whether to cook the suggestions. Recipe permissions cover the first year and allow us to replace individual selections without additional fees. Weekly support volume and editorial costs will be reviewed alongside the cohort results.
