# Access and participation research

District teams contacted 212 eligible adults before procurement. Seventy-eight can attend a regular classroom. Sixty-four have rotating work hours and can reliably take a telephone appointment but not a fixed classroom time. Forty-one lack dependable telephone access and need paper lessons with an occasional district drop-in. The remaining 29 did not enroll or were outside this course's reading range.

A prior classroom-only course lost 38 percent of learners after their work schedule changed. The consortium's later small trial combined paper, telephone and class records: 28 of 32 learners finished, including nine who changed route. Tutors used the same lesson sequence and recorded each completed activity. Learners said continuity mattered more than the appearance of the teaching channel.

The ordered 180 places reflect expected enrollment after assessment. District teams have current contact details, can distribute packs through community offices if post fails and already support large-print requests. Learners do not need a smartphone, home internet or their own printer. There is no fee for learners, and the consortium funds every purchased place.
