# LoopCrate collection membership

## Offer and outcome
LoopCrate already collects reusable shipping crates from neighborhood retailers on a per-collection basis. We will add a monthly membership so shops can arrange collections without approving a fresh invoice each time. The goal is to retain 40 shops that currently pause their account during busy periods and to run the membership as a profitable ongoing offer.

The member experience has three steps: select an available collection day, confirm the number of crates, and receive a collection receipt. Shops keep using the current crate labels and driver handover procedure. The membership serves only the central district, where we already operate daily routes. It does not add new towns or same-hour dispatch.

## Price and included service
Membership costs $19 per shop per month, including every requested weekday collection with no monthly count limit. The price remains $19 after the launch quarter. Existing per-collection customers can stay on their current tariff, but members pay no collection surcharge or minimum crate fee. Shops can cancel before the next billing month.

This simple price is the center of the offer. We will not attach it to packaging sales or require another purchase. The subscription is intended to stand on its own, with its own contribution report. We will invite shops that currently request collections throughout the month rather than only those with occasional returns.

## Dispatch foundation
Before membership opens, we will build a dedicated collection scheduler. It will accept a shop, district, requested date, and crate count; select a district route with sufficient remaining capacity; allocate a collection identifier; and produce the driver's manifest and the shop's receipt record. It will support next-day rescheduling and cancellation before dispatch.

Two engineers will spend seven weeks constructing and checking this service. Membership launch depends on completion of the new scheduler. We will keep the existing dispatch system running for per-collection accounts and reconcile both services in the daily route close. The member scheduler will use the same route capacities, operating days, and handover rules as current collections. It will not add a different optimization method or service window.

## Pilot operation
The first cohort is capped at 40 shops. Invitations go to current customers through account managers, who can activate membership under the existing business agreement. Drivers will see one combined manifest. The operations lead can suspend enrollment if expected collections exceed the district's staffed capacity, without changing benefits already sold.

We will review eight weeks of paid use. Success means at least 32 shops remain active, collection completion is at least 98%, and subscription receipts exceed recurring collection and account-service costs. We will compare retention against matched per-collection accounts and publish both totals and per-shop results internally.

## Resources
The approved construction budget is $62,000. Driver shifts and vehicles are already booked for the district, and the pilot consumes less than half the available collection slots. Customer support uses the current business desk. Finance owns the membership cost report, the dispatch lead owns route completion, and the product lead owns cancellation and renewal communications. A larger cohort needs a new operating and finance review.
