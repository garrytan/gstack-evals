# GateWren appointment repair

## What changes
GateWren manages collection appointments for freight depots. In the northern depot group, 23% of booked trucks are sent to the help desk because the gate cannot match their registration plate to an appointment. Dispatchers often substitute a vehicle after booking. We will reduce these help-desk diversions below 2% without weakening the depot's identity checks.

The repair assigns a stable appointment reference and lets an authorized dispatcher attach the current vehicle to it. At the gate, staff look up the reference, verify the authorized carrier and current vehicle, and admit the truck into its booked window. A vehicle change does not create another slot or transfer the appointment to a different carrier.

## Working flow
Dispatchers receive the reference through their existing booking channel. The current carrier contact can update a plate until fifteen minutes before arrival, after which the gate desk makes any change while checking the driver documents. The gate screen will show the old and new plate, who made the change, and the time. The depot's current identification rules remain unchanged.

We will add the stable reference and change record to the present appointment service, rather than replace scheduling. The first release covers eight depots using the same carrier directory. It excludes walk-in admission, new carrier enrollment, automatic identity approval, and changes to slot pricing. Every carrier keeps its existing dispatcher contact and support number.

## Build and partner rehearsal
Weeks 1 and 2 cover reference allocation, carrier lookup, and the plate-change record. Weeks 3 and 4 update the dispatcher notice and gate screen. Week 5 covers permission checks and training on recorded appointments. In week 6 we will finish depot configuration and installation rehearsals.

The first full-volume live rehearsal involving all eight depots and their external dispatch teams will take place in week 7, after construction is complete. It will use a peak morning's actual booking load. The depot lead will compare diversions, admission times, and identity exceptions with the existing process before authorizing the week-8 production switch. If any depot misses acceptance, that depot stays on its current service and receives the already arranged desk coverage.

## Rollout safeguards
We will switch two depots first and observe three mornings before switching the other six. Depot managers can return to the current gate process by disabling reference-led lookup; the booking record and original plate remain available. The contracted desk team will cover verification work through the first four weeks and can extend coverage under the signed rate. It can handle the full projected exception load, not only a sample.

## Measures and resources
The primary target is fewer than 2% help-desk diversions among booked trucks, with no increase in identity exceptions or average queue time. Reports include every booked truck, even those requiring manual handling. We will also record dispatcher time per substitution and carrier complaints. A depot will not count as improved by simply rejecting trucks before arrival.

Two engineers have eight weeks reserved. The $39,000 budget covers construction, training, and desk coverage at the documented peak load. The depot group has approved the operating process and the carriers have signed the update procedure. The product lead owns delivery, the depot security lead owns admission acceptance, and the operations analyst publishes results after each morning shift.
