# DockLark first service

## Customer result
DockLark will sell a managed arrival-warning service to regional grocery distributors. The first market is evening deliveries to small urban shops. We aim to cut failed refrigerated handoffs from 14% to below 5%, without changing the distributor's delivery price or the shop's opening schedule. Distributors pay $450 per depot each month for up to 60 receiving shops.

The founders will fund a twelve-week build and a four-week customer trial. Two distributors have agreed to supply operational contacts and take part in the trial. We will serve their existing routes rather than recruit new carriers or build a delivery marketplace.

## Service design
The service will turn carrier departure events into arrival warnings. A shop manager receives a text sixty minutes before the vehicle reaches the shop, then a second text at fifteen minutes. The depot desk sees the same timeline and can call a manager who has not acknowledged either message. This gives the shop enough notice to have someone at the receiving door and is the mechanism by which we will reduce failed handoffs.

Each shop has one primary contact and one alternate. Messages include the delivery reference, expected time, and depot callback number. The desk can correct a contact or suppress an alert after a delivery is cancelled. We will not offer storage, change route times, fund extended opening hours, or direct drivers to other addresses. Food handling and custody remain with the distributor.

## Work sequence
Weeks 1 through 3 produce the shop contact register and depot console. Weeks 4 through 7 add the carrier event feed, message scheduling, and a daily exceptions report. Weeks 8 through 11 cover desk training and supervised operation against recorded route days. Week 12 is the final acceptance rehearsal.

In week 13 we will offer the finished service to the two trial distributors at the published monthly price. Their procurement teams will then decide whether a separately billed warning service belongs in their depot budgets. We will use the four-week paid trial to establish purchase acceptance and renewal intent. The founder owns those negotiations; engineers stay available for corrections during the trial.

## Decision and reporting
The depot records every planned handoff, the actual arrival time, whether goods were accepted, and the reason for any failure. We will compare trial routes with the preceding four weeks and report both the raw rate and route-adjusted changes. A contract continues only if failed handoffs fall below 5%, delivery cost per accepted case does not rise, and the distributor renews at the stated price. Neither message opens nor acknowledgements count as successful deliveries.

## Limits and funding
The approved $74,000 covers two builders, a part-time operations lead, messaging, and founder-led sales. The production service uses a licensed event feed with known per-depot costs. There is no exclusivity or long-term vendor contract. The trial is capped at two depots and 120 shops. Additional depots require a separate funding decision after the trial results.
