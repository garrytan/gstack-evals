# PathMarsh refund recovery release

## The result we need
PathMarsh sells small-group rail holidays through local trip operators. Refund delays are the leading source of complaints after an operator cancels a departure. We will reduce the share of cancelled bookings taking more than seven days to repay from 46% to below 10% over the next four months. We will retain the same traveler audience, operator contracts, and booking fees.

The release has a $96,000 budget and three engineers for twelve weeks. An operations specialist will work half time on refund records and traveler communications. The existing payments provider, support desk, and operator relationships remain in place. We will not add a new payment method or charge travelers to receive money back.

## Faster traveler confirmation
The refund intervention will be a daily traveler confirmation flow. A cancelled booking opens a short task asking the traveler to confirm the receiving account and press ready for repayment. A daily message repeats the task until it is completed. Operations will see completed confirmations immediately and send the refund request through the current process that afternoon.

We will apply the task to every cancelled booking, including bookings with previously stored account details. A traveler can complete it without talking to support, and the desk can assist by telephone. This will shorten the path from cancellation to money reaching the traveler. We will keep the existing operator settlement schedule and keep repayment funded from the returned operator deposit.

## Complete trip workspace
The first release will also introduce a new traveler workspace: shared itineraries, group chat, a collaborative photo journal, saved station guides, and a points wallet for repeat-trip discounts. All five areas are required for the recovery release. We will ship the confirmation flow only when the entire workspace is ready, so every traveler receives a consistent account home rather than a series of separate launches.

The points wallet uses the already approved discount allowance. It is optional for travelers and has no effect on refund eligibility. We will not import old photo collections or build a public social feed. Group chat is limited to booked participants, with the current support team able to moderate reports. Station guides will use licensed text that the content team already maintains.

## Schedule and release controls
Weeks 1 and 2 cover refund task records and support tooling. Weeks 3 through 9 deliver the workspace areas in parallel, followed by two weeks of testing and a week of support rehearsal. The engineering estimate fits the staffed twelve-week window. The rollout begins with one operator and expands to the remaining six only after a month of refund outcomes meets the target.

## Evidence of improvement
Finance will report calendar days from cancellation to cleared traveler repayment, not the date a message or request was sent. The report will show the median, the share over seven days, the share over fourteen days, and exceptions by operator. We will also track repeat contacts per cancelled booking and total refund cost. Customer success will interview ten refunded travelers each month. The product lead owns the release, finance owns the repayment record, and the operations specialist can pause invitations if travelers receive conflicting instructions.
