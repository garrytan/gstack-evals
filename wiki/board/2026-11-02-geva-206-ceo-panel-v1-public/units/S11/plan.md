# Cinderbook collections desk

## Release purpose
Cinderbook helps wholesale suppliers manage invoices after delivery. This release adds a collections desk that links payment promises, invoice disputes and incoming bank credits. Account teams will see which balances require a conversation and which can receive an automatic reminder. We aim to reduce balances more than thirty days overdue without increasing the time the team spends collecting them.

The first cohort is thirty existing suppliers with two to six collections staff. The offer will cost $240 per month on top of the core invoice subscription. Customers retain their normal payment terms. We will not provide debt collection, lending or legal notices.

## Work in the desk
The desk will import bank credits each morning, attach matched payments to invoices and suppress reminders on settled balances. Staff can record a promised payment date or a dispute owner. A promise due today appears in a short call queue. An unresolved dispute stays with its assigned staff member rather than generating repeated generic messages.

Managers can choose reminder frequency within the limits already agreed with each buyer. The activity history records the underlying balance and the version of every message. We will retain the existing invoice view, so suppliers can return to their present workflow without moving data. All thirty launch accounts already support the required bank export.

## Partner reservation
In week one we will sign an eighteen-month, non-cancellable reservation with the matching service. It provides dedicated bank-file mapping staff and enough monthly volume for 150 suppliers. The total commitment is $210,000, paid quarterly. This will be the delivery foundation for the release rather than using Cinderbook's current batch-matching service in the first cohort.

The product pilot will open after the new desk is complete in week nine. We will then compare paid uptake at the $240 add-on price across the thirty invited suppliers. The reservation will remain in force for its full term regardless of the pilot's sales results.

## Team and delivery
Three engineers and one product lead have twelve weeks allocated. Weeks one to three cover the partner handoff and bank mappings. Weeks four to six deliver promises, disputes and the call queue. Weeks seven and eight add message controls and account setup. Weeks nine through twelve run the customer pilot and correct operational issues.

The approved $360,000 budget covers the full reservation, internal build and six months of support. Our existing account managers will invite customers during their quarterly review. Each supplier can approve the add-on under its current service agreement. We will limit invitations to accounts where a manager has confirmed staff time for the pilot.

## The operating scorecard
The release score will be the number of reminders delivered per eligible open invoice. We will call the release successful when that number rises 40% above the prior quarter across the cohort, with at least twenty suppliers renewing the add-on. The monthly product review will use that activity measure and renewal count as its two decision numbers.

We will also retain delivery failures, opt-outs and support minutes in the service log so operations can correct individual cases. Staff can stop reminders for any buyer. A customer can export its history and remove the add-on at the end of a monthly term. We will open the next cohort only after the scorecard threshold and twenty paid renewals have been reached.
