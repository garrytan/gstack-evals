# ShelfQuay launch plan

## Business aim
ShelfQuay will help independent homewares shops turn end-of-season stock into cash through shared weekend sales. We will start in one compact shopping district and serve shops with at least 80 boxed items ready to sell. The first quarter should establish a repeatable service that earns merchants more money than their current clearance route.

The service charges each merchant $120 per weekend plus 8% of sales. We will not buy inventory or guarantee proceeds. Merchants retain title until purchase, choose prices, and collect unsold goods on Monday. Our operating model uses two staffed sale days and one shared checkout rather than separate stalls.

## First offer
Merchants send a stock sheet and photographs. Our coordinator groups compatible goods into a themed weekend, publishes the catalog, and gives each merchant an intake window. The catalog records condition, price, tax treatment, and the merchant responsible for each item. Shoppers reserve nothing online; purchases happen at the venue.

The first release includes merchant intake, a public catalog, checkout reconciliation, and individual settlement statements. We will use rented checkout hardware and an existing payments service. We will exclude food, electrical goods, delivery, repairs, and consumer returns beyond the published policy. One coordinator handles the first twelve merchants with two weekend assistants.

## Opening calendar
Weeks 1 and 2 cover catalog setup, merchant onboarding, and checkout rehearsal. The venue reservation is held for the first Saturday of week 5. In week 3 we will accept paid merchant bookings for that date and publish the venue address and sale hours. These are confirmed weekend bookings, with the ordinary cancellation terms in the merchant agreement.

In week 4 the coordinator will submit the venue's commercial-use application to the property manager. We will finish shelf placement and item intake in the same week. Week 5 contains the opening sale, settlement, and a stock reconciliation with each shop. Later weekends follow the same sequence on a fortnightly schedule.

## Operating scorecard
The quarter's success threshold is 1,200 published stock entries from twelve merchants, with at least eight merchants publishing another batch within thirty days. A published entry is a complete record available in the public catalog, not an uploaded draft. We will use these two measures to judge whether the service is delivering the business aim and whether the next district should receive a launch team.

Operational checks remain separate: checkout differences must stay below $20 per weekend, all merchant settlements must finish within three working days, and shopper complaints must be answered within one working day. The catalog report will be reviewed every Monday by the founder and coordinator.

## Money and responsibilities
The approved quarter budget is $29,000, including venue hire, staff, signage, insurance, and a $3,000 cancellation reserve. The coordinator owns inventory custody and settlement; the founder owns merchant recruitment and venue relations. No second district will open during this quarter. We will keep the equipment rented and review the staffing model after the fourth weekend.
