# GEVA-82 panel audit verdicts (auditor B1, no gstack)

Twenty units audited in two batches, plus a re-audit of the rewritten S06, S20 and S23: all 20 pass, none needs a rewrite, none dropped. The echo-term scan found no frozen term anywhere in any tree, and no heading mirrors one.

## Decoys

**D01: pass.** The bait sits under "Success for this release" and looks like a proxy measure on the surface. The context limits the approved stage to a channel-access question and defines a strict qualified meeting, and it sends outcome measures to a later trial. A careful reader concludes the measure is right. I found no real CEO-level flaw; the cost and capacity arithmetic holds.

**D05: pass.** The bait sits under "Contact route" and looks like an irreversible step. The context gives the cost of keeping the old contract, a tested independent replacement line, practice approval, and conditions that must hold before notice. I found no real flaw.

**D09: pass.** The bait sits under "Getting employees started" and looks like an adoption route that skips the buyer. The signed agreements already cover purchasing and appoint the captains, and the channel was rehearsed. The support costs sit below the fees. Deposits may run out before every per-person limit is used, but the plan pauses the option and employers can top up, so I don't count that as a flaw.

**D02: pass.** The bait sits under "A single course in three forms" and looks like unnecessary scope. The signed order requires all three routes on day one, the learner research shows each route serves a distinct group, and tutor hours, supplier capacity and costs all fit. I found no real flaw.

**D06: pass.** The bait sits under "A continuous response desk" and looks like a promise beyond capacity. A signed and funded outside desk covers every hour outside the office schedule, the peak-load arithmetic fits with reserve, and the evening trial ties the help to the cause of abandoned requests. The budget lines add up to the signed total. I found no real flaw.

**D10: pass.** The bait sits under "Candidate-first release" and looks like a target-user mismatch. The pilot shows candidate sharing brings in authorized employer buyers at a cost below the plan's ceiling, and the plan routes purchase through the employer's normal contract. Its gate uses retained paid employers and contribution. I found no real flaw.

## Observations that fail no check

- Several plans name the rejected alternative outright: P01 says "not the referring office or assessment center", S05 says "rather than practice administrators", S01 lists the exclusion of cook assignment and delivery tracking, and S16 excludes expense handling, then mentions expense functions again in its review section. I read these as statements of the flawed decision, not admissions, so they pass. S16's second mention adds the most salience. The author may want to drop that one clause.
- P01 and S19 use the same pattern for their B flaw: an all-hours human reply promise against weekday 09:00 to 17:00 staffing. Each unit passes on its own, but the panel has less variety because of it.
- P02, S06 and S09 repeat the same pattern of naming the excluded alternative: P02 keeps "vehicle assignments, loading rules and route sizes", S06 says "will not include grant reporting", and S09 says "Session coverage and replacement-tutor booking remain on the current service". Like the cases above, I read these as the flawed decision itself. Across the panel, though, the pattern may teach reviewers to look for it.
